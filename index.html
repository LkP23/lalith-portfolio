About Me

I am beginning my Ph.D. journey in Management, with growing research interests at the intersection of computer networking, data analytics, and business decision systems.

I am especially interested in understanding how network performance, API ecosystems, and data flows shape real business outcomes such as service quality, operational risk, compliance, and decision-making.

My long-term goal is to explore how technical system-level signals can be translated into practical business intelligence for enterprise and digital platforms.

Research Interests

My current research interest is in bridging computer networking and management by studying how network-level observability can support better business decisions. I want to explore how network performance, API systems, and data movement affect outcomes such as revenue, risk, compliance, and service reliability. As I begin my Ph.D., I am interested in shaping this into a research direction that connects technical systems with business value.

Research Directions

These are the areas I hope to study as I begin my Ph.D. They reflect the questions I am most interested in exploring, rather than completed research projects.

Network Latency and Business Impact

I am interested in studying how API latency and network performance relate to business outcomes such as user experience, SLA compliance, and operational efficiency.

Over time, I would like to build models that connect technical performance metrics with business-facing indicators.

Areas of interest: Time-series analysis, performance monitoring, KPI modeling

Data Flow Traceability and Governance

I am also interested in how data moves across APIs and distributed systems, and how that movement can be made more transparent, auditable, and useful for governance.

This could lead to research on compliance, data lineage, and practical monitoring frameworks for enterprise systems.

Areas of interest: Data lineage, API monitoring, governance, compliance
